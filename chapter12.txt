INTRODUCTION
Background of Study

Aquarium management is an important practice in maintaining a healthy and balanced
aquatic environment. It involves the regular monitoring of water temperature, pH level,
turbidity, total dissolved solids, water level, and feeding schedules to ensure that
aquatic organisms remain within suitable environmental conditions. Water quality is a
major factor in fish health, growth, survival, and system stability because aquatic
organisms depend on specific environmental conditions to thrive [1]­[3]. When water
quality problems are not detected early, aquarium organisms may experience stress,
disease, poor growth, or mortality.

With the continuous advancement of technology, the Internet of Things (IoT) has
become an effective approach for environmental and aquatic monitoring. IoT-based
systems use sensors, microcontrollers, communication networks, databases, and
software platforms to collect and transmit data in real time. In aquaculture and water
quality monitoring, IoT systems have been used to measure important parameters such
as temperature, pH, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, and total dissolved solids, allowing
users to observe water conditions remotely and respond to abnormal readings more
efficiently [4]­[7]. These technologies support more consistent monitoring compared
with manual observation because sensor readings can be collected continuously and
stored for review.

The development of smart aquarium and aquaculture systems also supports broader
sustainability goals. The proposed project is aligned with Sustainable Development
Goal 14, Life Below Water, because it promotes the responsible monitoring and
management of aquatic environments. It also supports the research, development, and
extension direction of Davao del Norte State College by applying information technology
and IoT concepts to environmental monitoring, aquaculture-related learning, and
small-scale aquatic management. Through this alignment, the project demonstrates
how computing solutions can contribute to sustainable and technology-driven practices
in academic and local settings.

Despite these developments, many aquarium owners and caretakers still rely on
manual monitoring and routine inspection. Traditional aquarium maintenance requires
users to personally check water conditions, monitor water levels, feed fish, and respond
to possible problems. This process may become inconsistent because users may be
unavailable, may forget routine tasks, or may fail to notice sudden changes in aquarium
conditions. Studies on smart pond and aquaculture monitoring show that real-time
sensor-based systems can help address these limitations by providing continuous
readings, historical records, and decision support for water quality management [8], [9].

Existing IoT-based aquarium systems commonly include sensor-based monitoring,
automatic feeding, or remote access through web and mobile platforms. However,
several systems focus only on selected functions such as displaying sensor readings,
monitoring temperature, detecting water level, or controlling feeding schedules
[10]­[13]. Although these systems show the usefulness of IoT in aquarium
management, many of them do not fully integrate real-time monitoring, rule-based
automation, alert notification, data logging, aquarium records, user-based access,
power outage awareness, and bioload-based configuration in one centralized platform.

In the local context, aquarium systems are commonly used in households, schools,
laboratories, and small facilities for educational, recreational, and research purposes. At
Davao del Norte State College, aquarium setups may support academic activities,
demonstrations, and studies related to aquatic life, environmental monitoring, and
technology-based experimentation. However, these setups are often managed
manually, making them vulnerable to irregular checking, delayed response to abnormal
water conditions, and limited recording of historical data. Local users such as aquarium
hobbyists, students, caretakers, and laboratory personnel may also lack access to an
affordable and centralized monitoring tool that combines real-time data, automation,
alerts, and records in one system.

A review of related literature shows that there is a need for a more integrated aquarium
monitoring and automation system that is suitable for small-scale environments.
Rule-based algorithms are commonly appropriate for prototype monitoring systems
because they use predefined threshold values to trigger actions when sensor readings
move outside acceptable ranges. More advanced approaches such as fuzzy logic and
machine learning may improve classification and prediction, but these approaches
require additional datasets, calibration, and validation [14], [15]. For the current project,
a rule-based approach is more practical because the system needs to provide
immediate alerts and selected automated responses based on measurable aquarium
parameters.

Therefore, this study aims to develop SmartAqua Pro: IoT-Based Aquarium
Monitoring and Automation System. The proposed system is designed to monitor
selected aquarium parameters, including pH level, total dissolved solids, turbidity, water
temperature, and water level. It also aims to provide selected automation functions,
real-time alerts, data logging, aquarium records, power outage awareness,
bioload-based configuration, and a centralized web-based dashboard. Through this
system, aquarium owners, caretakers, students, and administrators may monitor
aquarium conditions more consistently, respond to abnormal readings more efficiently,
and improve the overall management of small-scale aquarium environments.

Significance of Study

The proposed SmartAqua Pro: IoT-Based Aquarium Monitoring and Automation System
is expected to provide significant benefits to different stakeholders by improving
monitoring consistency, automation, accessibility, and decision-making in small-scale
aquarium management.
Aquarium Owners and Hobbyists. This group will benefit the most from the system
because it provides real-time monitoring, automated support, and remote access to
aquarium conditions. The system enables users to track water temperature, pH level,
total dissolved solids, turbidity, and water level while receiving alerts when readings
become abnormal. This helps users respond earlier to potential problems and reduce
risks to aquatic life.
Aquarium Caretakers and Facility Personnel. Caretakers will benefit from the system
by reducing manual workload and improving consistency in aquarium maintenance.
Through automation, alerts, and centralized monitoring, they can manage routine
aquarium tasks more efficiently and check aquarium conditions without relying only on
physical inspection.
Schools and Academic Institutions. Educational institutions will benefit from the
system because it can support aquarium setups used for teaching, demonstration, and
research activities. The system provides sensor-based readings, historical records, and
reports that may be used for academic observation, experimentation, and technology
integration in learning environments.
IT Personnel and System Administrators. IT personnel and system administrators
will benefit from the centralized dashboard, user management, sensor logs, and system
activity records. These features can support easier maintenance, troubleshooting,
configuration, and monitoring of the web-based platform and connected IoT devices.
Researchers and Future Developers. This study will benefit researchers and future
developers by serving as a reference for projects related to IoT-based environmental
monitoring, smart aquarium systems, sensor-based automation, and water quality
monitoring. The system may also serve as a foundation for future enhancements such
as artificial intelligence, predictive analytics, mobile application integration, and
large-scale deployment.

Scope and Limitation

This study focuses on the design, development, and implementation of SmartAqua
Pro: IoT-Based Aquarium Monitoring and Automation System for small-scale
aquarium environments such as home aquariums, school laboratories, and selected
academic facilities. The system is intended for aquarium owners, caretakers, students,
IT personnel, and administrators who need real-time access to aquarium conditions. It
covers the integration of IoT-based sensors for monitoring pH level, total dissolved
solids, turbidity, water temperature, and water level, together with a centralized
web-based dashboard for viewing sensor readings, aquarium status, alert notifications,
user accounts, aquarium records, sensor logs, and monitoring history. The system also
includes real-time data collection, sensor data transmission, rule-based evaluation of
readings, alert generation, selected automation support, power outage awareness,
bioload-based configuration, and data logging to produce monitoring results, alert
notifications, aquarium status summaries, and historical records for user review and
decision-making.

The system is limited to monitoring selected aquarium parameters only, specifically pH
level, total dissolved solids, turbidity, water temperature, and water level. It can notify
users when readings are outside predefined threshold values, but it does not
automatically perform chemical correction, pH balancing, water treatment, or complete
water replacement. The automation component is limited to selected tasks supported by
the prototype hardware and predefined rules. The current prototype uses rule-based
automation and does not include artificial intelligence, machine learning, or predictive
analytics. The system also depends on stable internet connectivity for real-time data
transmission, remote access, and alert delivery. Furthermore, the system uses standard
IoT components and low-cost sensors; therefore, its readings may have limitations
compared with laboratory-grade or industrial-grade monitoring equipment. The system
is intended for small-scale aquarium environments and is not designed for large
commercial aquaculture operations, open-water fish farms, or fully automated water
treatment facilities.

Definition of Terms

Alert System - Refers to the feature that notifies users through the application when
abnormal aquarium conditions or system issues are detected.

Automation - Refers to the system's ability to perform selected tasks such as feeding
and alert-based responses without constant manual intervention based on predefined
conditions.
Bioload Configuration - Refers to the system setting that adjusts or guides monitoring
and automation parameters based on the number and type of aquatic organisms in the
aquarium.

Dashboard - Refers to the graphical user interface that displays real-time data, system
status, aquarium records, and alerts for user monitoring and control.

Data Logging - Refers to the process of recording and storing sensor data over time for
tracking, analysis, and reporting purposes.

Internet of Things (IoT) - Refers to the network of interconnected sensors and devices
used in the system to collect, transmit, and process aquarium data.

Power Outage Detection - Refers to the system feature that identifies interruptions in
power supply and sends notifications to the user.

Real-Time Monitoring - Refers to the continuous collection and display of aquarium
parameters such as pH level, total dissolved solids, turbidity, water temperature, and
water level as they occur.

Sensor - Refers to the hardware component used to measure environmental
parameters such as pH level, total dissolved solids, turbidity, water temperature, and
water level within the aquarium.

SmartAqua Pro - Refers to the IoT-based aquarium monitoring and automation system
developed in this study.

Threshold-Based System - Refers to the rule-based approach used in the system
where specific actions are triggered when sensor readings reach predefined limits.
Water Level Monitoring - Refers to the system function that tracks the amount of water
in the aquarium to help prevent overflow or depletion.

Water Quality Monitoring - Refers to the system capability to assess the condition of
water based on measurable indicators such as pH level, total dissolved solids, turbidity,
and temperature.

Water Temperature Monitoring - Refers to the system function that measures and
monitors the appropriate temperature range for the aquarium environment.

Web Application - Refers to the browser-based platform used for accessing the
system's monitoring, control, records, and reporting features.

METHODOLOGY

The project adopts a Hybrid Project Management Methodology that combines the
structured approach of the Waterfall model and the flexibility of Agile to effectively
support the development of the SmartAqua Pro system, as illustrated in Figure 1. The
Waterfall approach is applied during the initial stages, particularly in planning,
requirements definition, and documentation, ensuring that all outputs such as proposals
and system designs are clearly defined and validated before development begins.
Meanwhile, Agile practices are utilized during the implementation and testing phases,
allowing iterative development, continuous testing, and incremental improvement of
system features such as real-time monitoring, automation, alert mechanisms, and data
logging. This integration provides a balance between structure and adaptability, making
it suitable for systems with both well-defined requirements and evolving components
such as user interface enhancements and system optimization.

The project begins with the Planning phase, where the proponents defined the project
scope, objectives, timeline, and required resources based on the approved concept
proposal. During this phase, the overall direction of the SmartAqua Pro system was
established, including its key functionalities such as IoT-based monitoring, automation
of aquarium processes, and centralized management. The feasibility of the system was
also assessed to ensure that it can be completed within the given timeframe and
available resources.

In the Analysis phase, the proponents gathered and finalized system requirements by
reviewing existing aquarium management practices, identifying user needs, and
determining both functional and non-functional requirements. This includes identifying
the necessary sensors for monitoring water temperature, water level, and water quality,
as well as defining system features such as automation, alert notifications, mobile
access, and data logging.
In the Design phase, the proponents translated these requirements into a structured
system architecture. This includes the design of the database, user interface, and
overall system workflow. System diagrams, such as flowcharts and architectural
diagrams, were developed to illustrate how the system components interact. Prototypes
of the web and mobile interfaces were also created to visualize the user experience and
system functionality.

During the Implementation phase, the proponents developed the SmartAqua Pro
system using an iterative approach. This phase involved coding, integration of IoT
hardware components such as sensors and microcontrollers, and development of the
web and mobile applications. Features such as real-time monitoring, automated
feeding, lighting and filtration control, alert notifications, and data logging were
implemented and tested incrementally. Continuous testing and refinement were
conducted to ensure system accuracy, reliability, and responsiveness.

Finally, in the Maintenance phase, the proponents conducted system evaluation,
identified issues, and applied necessary improvements to enhance system performance
and usability. Although limited within the project duration, this phase ensured that the
SmartAqua Pro system remains stable, reliable, and capable of supporting future
enhancements such as additional sensors or advanced analytics features.

SYSTEM ANALYSIS
System Architecture
The system architecture shown in Figure 5 presents a structured framework that
illustrates how the different components of the SmartAqua Pro: IoT-Based Aquarium
Monitoring and Automation System interact to support real-time monitoring, automation,
and reporting. The architecture is composed of three main components: hardware (IoT
devices and sensors), software (application system and user interface), and data
management (server and cloud storage).
At the hardware level, the system utilizes IoT-based sensors such as water temperature
sensors, water level sensors, and water quality sensors installed within the aquarium
environment. These sensors continuously collect environmental data and transmit it
through communication protocols (e.g., MQTT or HTTP) to the central system. This
enables real-time data acquisition and ensures that changes in aquarium conditions are
immediately detected.
At the software level, the system includes a centralized application that processes
incoming sensor data using predefined threshold values and basic AI-based anomaly
detection techniques. The system analyzes the data to identify abnormal conditions and
automatically triggers alerts when necessary. A web or mobile-based dashboard is also
provided, allowing administrators and aquarium owners or caretakers to monitor
real-time data, receive alerts, and access system-generated reports.
The data management component consists of a centralized server and cloud storage,
where all sensor data, alerts, and historical records are stored and managed. This
enables efficient data retrieval, analysis, and report generation. The system supports
automated reporting features that summarize aquarium conditions, detected issues, and
system performance, supporting better decision-making and improved aquarium
management.

perform specific functions that support the operation, monitoring, configuration, and
maintenance of the system.

The System Administrator is responsible for managing and maintaining the system.
The administrator interacts with the system by logging in, viewing the dashboard,
managing user accounts, managing aquarium records, configuring threshold values,
viewing sensor logs and monitoring history, viewing alert history, and monitoring device
status. These functions allow the administrator to control system access, maintain
accurate aquarium records, set acceptable parameter ranges, and ensure that the IoT
components are functioning properly. The configuration and management data provided
by the administrator are processed and stored by the system to support reliable
aquarium monitoring and automation.

The User/Caretaker interacts with the system by logging in, viewing the dashboard,
managing aquarium records, configuring aquarium settings, configuring bioload, viewing
real-time sensor data, viewing aquarium status, viewing sensor logs and monitoring
history, viewing alert history, and receiving alerts. These interactions allow the user or
caretaker to monitor aquarium conditions, review previous readings, and respond to
abnormal water conditions. Through the dashboard and alert features, the system
provides updated information that helps users maintain suitable aquarium conditions
and respond promptly to possible issues.

The IoT Device/Sensor Module serves as an external actor that supplies
sensor-related data to the system. It interacts with the system by sending sensor
readings and detecting power interruptions. The transmitted data are processed by the
system and displayed through the dashboard as real-time monitoring information. When
sensor readings exceed the predefined threshold values or when a power interruption is
detected, the system generates alerts and updates the aquarium status. These
interactions demonstrate how SmartAqua Pro integrates user management, sensor data
processing, alert generation, and centralized monitoring to support efficient aquarium
management.

The User/Caretaker interacts with the system by providing login credentials, aquarium

SYSTEM DESIGN
Entity Relationship Diagram

The Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) illustrated in Figure 9 presents the database
structure of the SmartAqua Pro: IoT-Based Aquarium Monitoring and Automation
System, showing how data entities are organized and related to one another. In this
system, entities represent the major data components required for monitoring,
automation, and system management, including Users, Sensors, Sensor Data, Alerts,
Reports, and System Settings.

The Users entity stores information about system users, such as administrators and
aquarium owners or caretakers, including their login credentials, roles, and access
privileges. The Sensors entity represents the IoT devices installed in the aquarium,
which are responsible for collecting environmental data such as water temperature,
water level, and water quality parameters. Each sensor is associated with specific
configurations and identification details.

The Sensor Data entity stores the real-time readings collected from the sensors. These
data are continuously recorded and used by the system to monitor aquarium conditions
and evaluate whether they fall within predefined threshold values. This enables the
system to maintain consistent monitoring and support automated decision-making
processes.

Based on the processed sensor data, the system generates alerts, which are stored in
the Alerts entity. These alerts are linked to both the sensor data and the users who
receive notifications, allowing timely response to abnormal conditions. Additionally, the
Reports entity contains summarized information derived from historical sensor data and
alerts, supporting monitoring, analysis, and decision-making.

The System Settings entity stores configurable parameters such as threshold values,
automation rules, and notification preferences, which are defined and managed by the
system administrator. The relationships among these entities are established using
primary and foreign keys to ensure data integrity and consistency. For example, one
sensor can generate multiple sensor data records, while one user can receive multiple
alerts and access multiple reports, forming one-to-many relationships within the system.

Technologies, Concepts, and Theories

The SmartAqua Pro: IoT-Based Aquarium Monitoring and Automation System utilizes a
combination of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies, environmental monitoring
concepts, and rule-based decision-making models to enable real-time observation and
automation of aquarium conditions. The system follows a structured workflow that
processes data from acquisition to decision-making, ensuring accurate monitoring of
water quality and stable aquatic environments. By integrating sensing devices,
communication protocols, and web-based systems, the solution transforms raw
environmental data into meaningful information that supports efficient aquarium
management.

Data Collection

The system begins with data collection through IoT-based sensors installed in the
aquarium, including sensors for water temperature, pH level, turbidity, total dissolved
solids (TDS), and water level. These sensors continuously gather real-time
environmental data and transmit it to the system using communication protocols such
as MQTT or HTTP. This process applies the concept of continuous environmental
sensing in IoT systems, where physical conditions are converted into digital signals for
monitoring and analysis [1], [24]. Continuous data collection is essential to ensure that
the system can respond immediately to changes in aquarium conditions, allowing users
to maintain safe and stable aquatic environments.

Data Pre-Processing

The collected data then undergoes pre-processing, where noise filtering, handling of
missing values, and standardization of sensor readings are performed to ensure
reliability. Raw sensor outputs may contain inconsistencies due to environmental factors
or hardware limitations; thus, pre-processing is necessary to ensure that only valid and
accurate data is used for system evaluation. This stage improves data quality and
ensures more reliable monitoring results [6], [12].

Feature Extraction

After pre-processing, feature extraction is conducted to identify meaningful aquarium
parameters that influence water quality and aquatic health. These include water
temperature, pH level, turbidity, total dissolved solids, and water level. These
parameters serve as key indicators of aquarium conditions and are commonly used in
water quality monitoring systems to assess environmental stability [3], [7]. By extracting
these relevant features, the system can determine whether aquarium conditions remain
within safe operational thresholds.

Classification

The system then applies classification and decision-making processes using a
rule-based threshold algorithm. This algorithm evaluates the collected sensor
readings by comparing each value with predefined minimum and maximum threshold
values for key aquarium parameters, including water temperature, pH level, turbidity,
total dissolved solids, and water level. Through this process, the system determines
whether the current aquarium condition is within the acceptable range or requires user
attention.

The rule-based threshold algorithm follows a simple conditional evaluation model:

If sensor value < minimum threshold  Low Alert
If sensor value > maximum threshold  High Alert
If minimum threshold  sensor value  maximum threshold  Normal Condition

This evaluation framework enables SmartAqua Pro to detect abnormal aquarium
conditions in real time and generate appropriate responses such as alert notifications,
aquarium status updates, and selected automation actions. Rule-based monitoring is
suitable for IoT-based water quality and aquarium monitoring systems because it
provides fast decision-making, requires low computational resources, and does not
depend on large training datasets [4], [6], [21]. These characteristics make the
rule-based threshold algorithm appropriate for SmartAqua Pro, particularly since the
system is designed as a prototype focused on real-time monitoring, alert generation,
and selected automation for small-scale aquarium environments.

Based on the classification results, the system provides outputs such as water quality
alerts, monitoring updates, feeder or filtration activity records, and real-time aquarium
status information displayed through the web-based dashboard.

Technologies Used in the System
Internet of Things (IOT)

The SmartAqua Pro system primarily relies on Internet of Things (IoT) technology, which
enables continuous monitoring and automation of the aquarium environment. The
system uses interconnected sensors installed within each aquarium to collect essential
data such as water temperature, water level, pH, turbidity, and total dissolved solids
(TDS). These sensors operate in real time and transmit data to a central processing
system for evaluation and decision-making.
Data communication between the sensors and the central system is handled through
lightweight protocols such as MQTT or HTTP, ensuring efficient, reliable, and
low-latency transmission. Through IoT integration, SmartAqua Pro can perform
functions such as automated feeding, filtration control, water quality monitoring, and
real-time alert generation when parameters exceed predefined thresholds. This
real-time capability enhances aquarium management and supports timely intervention
to maintain optimal aquatic conditions.
